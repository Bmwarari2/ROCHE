Notes:

- The photo in the point-of-care header (B1) is a safety control against wrong-person selection (hazard HZ-001), not an optional feature, so it is not consent-based. Photos shared with families (B8) are.
- The national data opt-out is flagged per person and honoured for any use beyond direct care (for example reporting to the NHS).
- The hospital transfer pack and the transfer of care summary are disclosures for direct care under B1 to the receiving hospital or provider, audited with the recipient. Death notifications to the regulator, the medical examiner and LeDeR are B7.
- Family members, representatives and professionals see a section of a person's record only under a recorded sharing decision ([people.md](../specs/people.md) rule 13): photos and updates under consent (B8); care plan sections, health summaries and finance under the person's consent, a legal authority (lasting power of attorney, deputyship, appointeeship), a best-interests decision or direct care. Which Article 6 and Article 9 conditions apply to sharing under a legal authority or best-interests decision is for the DPO to confirm `[DPO - human action]` (H-38).
- Consent records (B1) record the person's decisions about their care and who made them; they are not consent as a lawful basis, which stays limited to B3a and B8 to B10 (master prompt 5.1). The identification photo on the shared-device picker is B3 (employment), never B3a.
- Data subject requests (B5) meet the controller's duties under UK GDPR Articles 12 to 22: a legal obligation, never consent. The workbench highlights other people's names for the reviewer but never redacts or withholds on its own; a person decides each record with a reason (DPA 2018 Schedules 2 to 4; ADR-0090), and erasure is assessed against retention and recorded, with deletion left to the retention engine. Portability applies to staff records (contract), not to care records.
- Single sign-on (B3, B5) authenticates staff for employment and security; it is never a lawful basis of its own. It links only members the organisation already invited, never creates one, and the organisation's own second factor rules still apply (ADR-0091).
- Incidents may hold third-party data about witnesses, staff and visitors. A staff injury is also B3, and RIDDOR reports are B7. Disclosure to CQC, HSE, local authorities, the police, UKHSA, LeDeR and the medical examiner is a legal obligation (Article 6(1)(c), or the B4 and B7 conditions) recorded on the notification with its reference ([safety.md](../specs/safety.md) section 8). A reader who can see only some of the people on an incident sees the header and their own people ([ADR-0033](../decisions/0033-incident-scope.md), H-70).
- The safeguarding log (B4; [safety.md](../specs/safety.md) "Safeguarding log") is read in full only by holders of `safety.safeguarding.manage`; staff caring for the person read only the protective measures, never the allegation; the log is never sent to devices. Referrals to the local authority and to the DBS (Safeguarding Vulnerable Groups Act 2006 s.35) are disclosures under a legal duty, recorded on the concern with their reference. An alleged perpetrator named in the free text is a data subject: a subject access request about them is handled through the DSAR workbench with the safeguarding exemptions considered (H-596). Kept as `serious_incident`.
- Disclosure requests ([safety.md](../specs/safety.md) section 11, SAFE-016) are disclosures of one person's records to a coroner, a Safeguarding Adults Review, a Domestic Homicide Review, the police, a solicitor or, after the person's death, their personal representative under the Access to Health Records Act 1990. Each request stores the legal basis for its type (`legalBasis`): a legal obligation for coroners (Coroners and Justice Act 2009, Schedule 5), Safeguarding Adults Reviews (Care Act 2014, sections 44 and 45) and Domestic Homicide Reviews (Domestic Violence, Crime and Victims Act 2004, section 9), Article 6(1)(c) with the B4 or B7 special category conditions; the DPA 2018 Schedule 2 paragraph 2 crime exemption or paragraph 5 legal proceedings exemption for the police and solicitors, which needs a stated purpose; the 1990 Act for deceased people, who are outside UK GDPR. The DPO confirms the bases (H-592, H-821). Records are found by the DSAR collector (ADR-0090) and reviewed one by one by a person, who discloses, redacts named fields or withholds with a reason; nothing is redacted automatically. Someone other than the person who logged the request approves it, and release needs a fresh step-up. The pack as issued is kept in the exports bucket (eu-west-2, SSE-KMS) under `tenants/<tenant>/disclosures/<request>/`, with its SHA-256 on the request. The audit trail holds ids, the type, counts and the hash, never who asked or why. Requests, items and packs are kept under `serious_incident` until a class of their own is agreed (H-820). The disclosure log itself is never part of a pack; a subject access request still finds it.
- Family feed (comms.md): photos in the feed rest on consent from the person or a representative with authority (B8); where the person lacks capacity to decide, a best-interests decision is the alternative, and which Article 6 and Article 9 conditions then apply is for the DPO (H-38, H-106). Notifications carry an approved generic line and a link only, so the email provider, the SMS gateway and push services never receive update content. Notification delivery logs follow the class of the record they relate to until the DPO sets a shorter period (H-106).
- Withdrawing photo consent (or the end of the best-interests basis) stops sharing at once: photo updates, and any update carrying a photo, are taken down so family members no longer see them (`photoItemsToWithdraw`), and no new photo is composed. The feed items, the photo documents and the record of who saw them are not deleted; they stay in the care record under `care_record` as the history of what was shared, which a DSAR, a complaint or the breach register may need. Copies a family member already saved cannot be recalled, and the consent wording says so (H-106).
- Family members sign in to a portal session that reaches only the feed and their own rows (ADR-0061); they are never given a person assignment, so no staff table is open to them.
- Dependency categories (B1, Phase 2 slice 12) are recorded to plan the care each person needs and to staff the service to it (Regulation 18 of the 2014 Regulations); they are chosen by staff from the organisation's own scale, never derived by Harbour, and no decision about the person is made by the software (UK GDPR Arts 22A to 22D). The lawful basis and special category condition are B1's; the DPO confirms them and the retention of the scale versions that rotas rest on (H-711).
- Pre-admission assessments of people who are never admitted have no retention period of their own yet; until one is set (H-39) they follow `care_record`, counted from the date the enquiry closed.
- Inspector evidence packs are disclosures to a regulator or commissioner under B7: Article 6(1)(c) where CQC requires the information (Health and Social Care Act 2008 s.64), otherwise the basis of the regulated activity in B1; special category condition Article 9(2)(h) with DPA 2018 Schedule 1 paragraph 2(2)(f) ("the management of ... social care systems or services"). Minimised by rule ([quality.md](../specs/quality.md) rules 2 to 4), recipient-bound and time-limited (ADR-0050); retention `evidence_pack`. The DPO confirms the basis, the redaction rules and the defaults (H-96).
- Downtime packs are copies of B1 data for continuity of care during outages; printed copies are kept securely and shredded when replaced (deployer control in the business continuity plan). Retention `downtime_pack`, or `downtime_record` when in force during an outage. Opening, building or printing a whole-home pack is treated as a bulk export: it needs a fresh sign-in (step-up), is limited per member per hour (`downtime_pack.requests_per_hour`) and is audited; a reused pack is not copied again (#45 and #46 review). The printed PDF is drawn when asked for and sent as a download marked not to be cached, named by the pack id only, never stored in Harbour; the print is audited once it is drawn. Photos are not printed (H-211), so each page tells staff to confirm who the person is.
- Deaths and missing persons (slice 37): the death record and its mortality review are B1 (the
  care record and the governance of care, Article 9(2)(h)); the notifications they follow are B7.
  The missing person timeline is B1; giving the Herbert Protocol profile to the police when a person
  is missing is proposed as Article 6(1)(d) vital interests (or 6(1)(e) for a public body), with
  Article 9(2)(c) where the person cannot consent, or DPA 2018 Schedule 1 paragraph 18
  (safeguarding of individuals at risk); the DPO confirms (H-772). Profiles are prepared with the
  person and their family, kept as `care_record`, and say where any prefilled part came from.
- Letters are disclosures to the person's contacts under B1 or B2 (candour letters under B7, regulation 20 of the 2014 Regulations); special category content, the NHS number and the date of birth go by letter, never in an email body, and incident and candour letters are letters only (ADR-0051, H-99). A candour letter goes only to the relevant person under regulation 20(7) (H-103). Letters are kept as `care_record`, or `serious_incident` when about an incident graded serious.
- The free full export ([platform-completions.md](../specs/platform-completions.md) rules 10 to 16) is the controller's own copy of its data, not a disclosure to a third party. It goes only to the owner who asked for it, after a fresh sign-in with MFA, until it expires (never beyond `export.link_expiry_hours_max`), from the europe-west2 export bucket under the organisation's own prefix, and every step is audited (hazard HZ-070). The archive is fetched once: the download is recorded before the bytes stream out and the worker then deletes the archive (retried until it is gone); a second fetch is refused and a new export asked for (it is free). The web export page passes the download through the web tier (UK region only, ADR-0001) as a stream marked not to be cached or stored, so no copy is kept there. The archive is kept in the exports bucket (Google Cloud europe-west2, Cloud Storage with customer-managed encryption keys, lifecycle expiry; set today by `S3_EXPORTS_BUCKET` until the Cloud Storage adapter replaces the S3 one, [ADR-0098](../decisions/0098-production-on-google-cloud.md)) under `tenants/<tenant>/exports/<export>/`, never in the documents bucket. When it is ready, or if it fails, the requester is told in the app and by email, with the export's id only and a link to the export page; the email goes through the organisation's email provider like every notification. Only columns classified as exported leave; passwords, sessions, secret references and storage keys never do. Sample data is left out.
- Search audit events record what kinds of record were searched, the searcher's scope and the opaque ids of what was shown, never the words typed: they are usually a name, and a hash of a name can be reversed by trying names. A search that showed people is a view of their care records and is kept as long as those records (`audit_event.care_record`). The typed reason for a break-glass search is kept once, in the break-glass record, not copied into the audit trail.
- Break-glass search ([platform-completions.md](../specs/platform-completions.md) rule 9) is processing for B1, direct care when a person must be found outside the searcher's access (an emergency, covering another home), with B5 accountability: the same Article 6 and Article 9 bases as B1. It discloses identity only (names, date of birth, NHS number, status) to the searcher, never where the person lives; the typed reason is kept once, in the break-glass record, under `audit_event`. Recipients: the searcher; the Registered Manager of each home whose people were shown and the data protection lead, told at once in generic words; both again in the weekly break-glass report (master prompt section 4), which names the searcher (display name and job title) to its readers. A search that showed no one at any home is reported only to readers whose access reaches the whole organisation (the data protection lead, the owner), never named to a home's manager (review round 1 of the report screen). An alert that cannot be delivered (no Registered Manager at a home, or no active data protection lead) is audited, not dropped.
- Complaints (Phase 2, slice 18) are handled under Regulation 16 of the 2014 Regulations, a legal obligation: Article 6(1)(c) for an independent provider and a public body alike, with Article 9(2)(h) and Schedule 1 paragraph 2 where a complaint reveals health; they are filed under B1 (the care the complaint is about) and B7 (regulatory duties) until the controller decides whether complaints need a purpose row of their own (H-700). Compliments rest on Article 6(1)(f) legitimate interests (recognising good care and evidencing quality); the sender's name is kept as they gave it. Neither is ever shared outside the organisation by Harbour; the evidence library (slice 14) links a compliment by id.
- The import wizard (platform.md rules 18 to 26, ADR-0092) moves the controller's own records from
  its previous system into Harbour under the purpose of the records imported (B1 for people; B2
  and B3 as contacts and staff imports land); it adds no purpose. Data minimisation: only columns
  matched to a Harbour field are kept, staged rows stay in quarantine for at most
  `import.staging_expiry_days` and are deleted at commit, discard or expiry, and no error, log or
  audit event carries an imported value. Only a member with the import permissions, and the right
  to read people, across the whole organisation may import, so matching never discloses a person
  at another home or one the importer could not otherwise see (HZ-130).
  The DPO confirms the staging period and that the DPIA covers the migration (H-681, R26).
  The web wizard reads the file in the importer's browser to show its column headings and first
  three rows beside each field (HZ-130); only the upload sends it, through the web tier's own
  upload route (UK region only, ADR-0001) to the API, as the request body, never stored, cached or
  logged on the web tier. Nothing in the address or a log names a person (H-890).
- Whistleblowing and speaking up (Phase 2, slice 24; B16) sits in a silo (ADR-0089): only the Freedom to Speak Up lead and the members they name on a case read it, never the owner, the Registered Manager or anyone with a grant, and it is left out of the free full export (the lead exports it separately, H-721). An anonymous concern stores nothing that identifies who raised it: no account, IP address or device, in the record, audit, outbox or logs; its reply token is stored only as a SHA-256. What someone writes may still identify them, and the guidance to reporters says so (H-722). The DPO reviews who holds the lead role (H-720). Audit events about a concern are read only by the lead, so their times cannot be lined up with anything by a wider role. To hold back floods, public requests are counted per network: the network (an IPv6 /64 or IPv4 address) is kept only as an HMAC keyed by a managed secret and the date, for the current minute, and swept hourly. Subject access collection lists the silo as not searched; the lead answers for it separately.
