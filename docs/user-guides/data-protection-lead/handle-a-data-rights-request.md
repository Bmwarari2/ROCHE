# How to handle a data rights request

A data rights request is someone asking to see, correct, erase or take their information. Harbour
keeps the clock for you, finds the records, and helps you review each one before you respond.

**Who can do this**: the owner and the quality lead, or anyone whose role covers data protection
for the whole organisation. A Registered Manager or deputy for one home does not see **Data
rights**.

**Register row**: PLAT-022.

## Record the request

1. Go to **Data rights** in the menu. It opens on **Requests**.
2. Under **Record a request**, choose **What they are asking for** and **Whose information it is**
   (a person we support or a member of staff), then choose who.
3. Under **Who is asking**, choose **The person themselves** or **Someone on their behalf**. For
   someone on their behalf, enter their name and choose **Their authority to ask**, such as a
   lasting power of attorney.
4. Choose **How it arrived** and the **Day it arrived**.
5. Leave **We need to confirm who is asking before we respond** ticked unless you already know
   who they are.
6. Choose **Record the request**. The request opens.

## Follow the clock

The **Clock** shows where the request stands, as an icon and a word:

| You see | It means |
| --- | --- |
| Clock not started | Confirm who is asking. The clock starts that day. |
| Running: Respond by 01/12/2026 | Respond by that day. |
| Due soon | The day is close. |
| Overdue | The day has passed. Respond now. |
| Paused | You are waiting for the requester to say what they want. |
| Responded | The request is closed. It says if the response was late. |

Harbour works the date out each time you open the page, from the dates you record, the bank
holidays and your organisation's settings.

## Do the work

1. **1. Confirm who is asking**: choose how you confirmed it and the day, then **Confirm and start
   the clock**.
2. **Pause or extend the clock**: pause only while you wait for the requester to clarify; choose
   **Restart the clock** when they do. Extend only for a complex request or several from the same
   person, and say why.
3. **2. Find the records**: choose **Find the records**. Read **Not searched: check these
   yourself** first. Harbour could not search those parts of the record for this person, so check
   each one by hand.
4. **3. Review each record**: choose **Review** beside each record. You will be asked to confirm it
   is you (step-up). Harbour marks other people's names and lists them under **Other people
   named**. Choose **Send it as it is**, **Send it with parts taken out** (tick the parts and give
   the reason) or **Hold it back** (give the reason). Harbour never takes anything out for you.
5. **4. Send the response**: once every record is decided, choose **Check the response and send
   it**. Read it, choose **Download the response to send**, and send it to the requester.
6. Under **Record the response**, choose the **Outcome** and the day, then **Record the response
   and close the request**. A late response stays marked late.

After that, **Download exactly what was sent** gives you the same file again.

## Erasure and portability

- For erasure, choose **Check what can be erased**. Harbour shows what can go, what must be kept
  for now (with the date) and what is under a legal hold. It deletes nothing itself.
- For taking information elsewhere, Harbour says whether the right applies. It does not apply to
  care records; offer access instead.

## Good to know

- A request from a person's contact (such as a relative) about their own information, and
  recording which records were corrected, are not on these screens yet. Ask Harbour support.
- A record that no longer exists cannot be decided here yet. Tell Harbour support.
- The procedure is in your data protection policies; Harbour supports your judgement and never
  replaces it.
