# How to record and handle a data breach

Every personal data breach goes in the breach register, whether or not it must be reported to the
ICO. Harbour keeps the ICO clock and suggests what to consider. The decision is yours.

**Who can do this**: the owner and the quality and compliance lead, or anyone whose role covers
data protection for the whole organisation. A Registered Manager or deputy whose role covers one
home does not see the register.

**Register row**: PLAT-022.

## Steps

### Record it

1. Go to **Data rights**, then **Breaches**.
2. Under **Record a breach**, describe **What happened** without naming anyone. The register is
   not a care record.
3. Enter **When did the organisation become aware of it?**: when you were reasonably sure a breach
   had happened, in UK time on the 24-hour clock.
4. Enter **When did it happen? (optional)** if you know. Leave it blank if you do not.
5. Choose **Record breach**. The breach opens and says "Breach recorded."

### Work through it

Open the breach from **Breaches recorded**. Each step is shown only while it applies.

1. **Assessment**: answer **How many people are affected?** and **Tick each one that applies**,
   then choose **Save assessment**.
2. **Harbour's suggestion**: read it, and **Why Harbour suggests this:**. It is decision support
   only.
3. **Decision**: answer **Will you report this breach to the ICO?** and **Will you tell the people
   affected?**, and write **Your reasons**. If you decide differently from the suggestion, tick
   "I have read Harbour's suggestion and I am deciding differently". Then choose
   **Record decision**.
4. **Report to the ICO**: once you have reported it to the ICO yourself, enter **When did you report
   it?** and the **ICO reference**, then **Record report**. After the deadline, you must also give
   the **Reasons for the delay**.
5. **Telling the people affected**: Harbour fills in the **Notice to the people affected** from
   the register. Write every part in square brackets, send it, enter **When were they told?**, then
   choose **Record notice**.
6. **Closing**: write the **Lessons learned**, tick "Close this breach. It cannot be changed
   afterwards.", then choose **Close breach**.

## Read the ICO clock

Each breach shows the clock in words, with an icon:

| You see | It means |
| --- | --- |
| ICO report due: "Report to the ICO by 14:00 on 02/11/2026: 20 hours left" | The deadline, if it must be reported. |
| ICO report overdue: "Overdue: the ICO deadline passed at ..." | Report now, and give the reasons for the delay. |
| Reported on time, or Reported late | The report is recorded. |
| No report needed | Your decision is not to report it to the ICO. |

The hours come from your organisation's settings, counted from when the organisation became aware,
nights and weekends included.

## Good to know

- **Harbour never reports to the ICO, and never tells the people affected.** It shows the clock
  and drafts the notice. You report and send, then record that you did.
- **Harbour's suggestion never decides.** You can go against it; your reasons should say why.
- You can change the decision until the report or the notice is recorded. A change is kept in the
  record's history with its own reasons.
- A closed breach cannot change.
- Correcting what happened or when, after recording, is not on this screen yet. Ask Harbour
  support.
- If Thapsus tells you of a breach as your processor, see
  [How to acknowledge a breach notice from Thapsus](acknowledge-a-breach-notice-from-thapsus.md).
- Your procedure is the [breach response procedure](../../compliance/breach-response.md). The
  rules are in the
  [platform specification](../../specs/platform.md#breach-register-and-data-protection-complaints-slice-20-plat-022).
  Harbour supports your judgement and never replaces it.
