# How to speak up anonymously

Raise a concern without signing in and without giving your name. Anyone can use this page: staff,
agency workers and visitors.

**Who can do this**: anyone with the page's address. You do not need a Harbour account.

**Register row**: SAFE-012.

## Find the address

1. If you work here, sign in and choose **Speak up** in the menu.
2. Under **Raise a concern anonymously**, the page shows the address. Choose **Copy the address**
   and share it with colleagues, agency staff and visitors. The page says "Address copied". If it
   says "Copying did not work", select the address and copy it yourself.

## Steps

1. Open the address. The page is called **Speak up anonymously**.
2. Read **What anonymous means here** first.
3. Under **Your concern**, choose **What it is about** and write **What happened**: what you saw or
   heard, when and where.
4. If you want to read replies, tick **I want a reply code so I can read replies**. Without one,
   nobody can tell you what happened.
5. Choose **Send anonymously**. The page says **Your concern was sent** and the date it arrived.
6. If you asked for one, the page shows your **Reply code**, and the date it works until. **Write
   down or copy your reply code now. It will not be shown again.**

## Read replies

1. Open the same address.
2. Under **Read replies**, enter your **Reply code** and choose **Read replies**.
3. You see the state of your concern, any messages and, when the lead has finished, **What
   happened**.
4. To write back, use **Reply** and choose **Send**.

## Good to know

- You do not sign in, and you are not asked for your name.
- Harbour does not keep your IP address, your device or a cookie, and the page stores nothing in
  your browser. To stop misuse it counts requests from your network for a minute, as a scrambled
  code that cannot be turned back into your address, deleted within the hour.
- What you write may still show who you are. Leave out what you do not want known.
- Anyone with your reply code can read the replies, so keep it safe. If you lose it, nobody can
  show it to you again.
- Only the Freedom to Speak Up lead, and people they name to look into it, read your concern.
- If something is putting a person in danger now, tell the person in charge or call the
  emergency services. The lead is told in Harbour that a concern has arrived,
  naming nobody, but may not read it straight away.
- You can also speak up outside the organisation, to the National Guardian's Office or a prescribed
  person such as the Care Quality Commission.
- The rules: [safety specification, web screens](../../specs/safety.md#web-screens-phase-2-slice-62)
  and [ADR-0089](../../decisions/0089-restricted-silos-whistleblowing.md).
