# Operations (`operations`) - specification

Master prompt section 13. Phase 1 builds one operations feature: the nightly downtime pack with the
back-entry workflow (section 13.4, fire safety and PEEPs row: "a nightly downtime pack per location
(MAR, care plan summaries, allergies, PEEPs, contacts) printed or cached on the kiosk for power cuts
and IT outages, with downtime forms and a back-entry workflow"). The rest of section 13 (occupancy,
domiciliary scheduling, supported living, facilities, catering, front door, day services) arrives in
Phases 3 and 4. Status per feature: [feature register](feature-register.md).

## 1. Purpose and register rows

When the power, the network or Harbour fails, staff still know who is in the building, what they
are allergic to, every resuscitation decision in force, what texture of food and drink is safe, how
each person communicates and how to get them out; and when systems return, the care written on
paper goes into the record with its true time, marked so nobody mistakes it for a contemporaneous
entry or records it twice.

| Phase 1 rows | Later rows |
| --- | --- |
| OPS-028 nightly downtime pack with back-entry workflow | Fire risk assessment actions and PEEPs (OPS rows for 13.4, Phase 3), which replace the PEEP placeholder; the eMAR (Phase 2), which replaces the medicines placeholder; every other section 13 row |

## 2. Schema (for the schema and API step)

Forced RLS on every table (ADR-0002). An outage is visible to staff at its location and to
tenant-wide roles; a downtime pack follows its location, and its content follows the people in it
(a user sees a pack only if they may see every person on it, which `operations.downtime.read` holders
at the location can).

| Table | Kind | Key columns | Retention class |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `downtime_pack` | append-only | location, pack id, source (`server_nightly`, `server_on_demand`, `kiosk_cache`), `generated_at`, `as_at`, `valid_until`, generated by (system or user), content snapshot (JSON from `assembleDowntimePack`), rendered PDF document, warnings summary (counts and opaque ids only) | `downtime_pack`; a pack in force during a declared outage moves to `downtime_record` |
| `outage` | mutable, versioned | location, kind (`OUTAGE_KINDS`), description, `started_at`, `ended_at`, `declared_at`, declared by, declared late (flag), `closed_at`, closed by, `back_entry_due_at`, workflow instance (`OUTAGE_WORKFLOW`), reconciliation (by, at, statement, reviewed entry ids, forms entered), cancellation reason | `downtime_record` |
| Back-entry columns on the append-only care events (`care_note`, `observation`, `body_map_mark`, `activity_record`, `visit_log`) | expand only | `late_entry_reason` (`downtime` or null), `outage_id`, `paper_date` and `paper_time` as written, `paper_occurrence` (`only`, `earlier`, `later`), `outside_outage_reason`, `paper_reference`, `not_from_paper_reason` | `care_record` (unchanged) |
| Location settings | tenant configuration | `downtime.mar_location`, `downtime.peep_location` (where the paper MAR and the PEEPs are kept, worded for the page) | tenant configuration |

The care events already store the device time and the server-received time; the back-entry label
shows both. Prints, downloads, kiosk caching and opens of a pack are audit events, not a table.
Field mapping: [data-standards.md](data-standards.md#packs-and-governance-phase-1-slice-11);
purposes: [records of processing](../compliance/records-of-processing.md).

## 3. Permissions

From `packages/domain/src/platform/permissions.ts` (matrix: [permissions.md](permissions.md)).

| Permission | Allows | Held by (built-in roles) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `operations.downtime.read` | Print or open the location's downtime pack; unlock the kiosk's cached pack in a PIN session | Owner, Registered Manager, deputy, clinical lead, team leader |
| `operations.downtime.manage` | Declare, close, reconcile and cancel outages | As above |
| `care.note.write` and the other care write permissions | Back-entering a paper record (the same permission as writing it at the time) | As for the record kind |

Care workers and agency staff read the printed pack the nurse or senior in charge hands out; they
do not hold location-wide read access in Harbour, because their scope is the people assigned to
them. Reconciling an outage needs `operations.downtime.manage` and someone who entered none of its
paper records.

## 4. Rules that are easy to get wrong

Every rule has a table-driven test in `packages/domain/src/operations/` named with its hazard ID.
Figures come from `rate_table` by key, resolved with `resolveOperationsRates`; the downtime figures
are policy defaults (marked to verify), never statutory.

1. **Content** (`DOWNTIME_PACK_SECTIONS`). For each person: name, date of birth, NHS number, room
   and photo reference; allergies (most severe first, unknown severity with the severe ones);
   resuscitation status; the IDDSI food level and the IDDSI drink level, each with number, name and
   colour; communication needs; the PEEP; medicines due while the pack is in use; key contacts; the
   active care plan summarised by need (domain order, with contingencies); active risk assessments.
   The first eight are safety-critical (HZ-021): never blank. A missing one reads "not recorded" in
   words, with what to do ("IDDSI drink level not recorded. Check with the nurse in charge before
   giving any drink."), goes into `missingCritical` and onto the front sheet; a positive "none" is
   shown as recorded. Drafts and superseded care plans never print (HZ-024).
2. **Freshness** (HZ-021). Every pack carries `generated_at`, `as_at` (when the information was
   read: the server's read time, or a kiosk's last sync) and `valid_until` = `as_at` plus
   `downtime_pack.valid_hours`, which is longer than the nightly interval so one failed night still
   leaves an in-date pack. Every page's header is worked out when the page is printed or shown
   (`downtimePageHeader`), in this order:
   - the showing device's clock is earlier than `as_at` by more than `sync.clock_drift_flag_minutes`:
     "Check this device's clock ... treat the pack as possibly out of date";
   - an outage is declared and open at the location: "Latest available pack: information as at ...
     Anything recorded since ... is on paper or not shown here: check the downtime forms and ask the
     nurse in charge about changes.";
   - before `valid_until`: "Information as at ... Use until ..., then use a newer pack.";
   - after it: "Out of date: this information is from ... (N hours ago) and should have been
     replaced by ... check allergies, resuscitation status and diet with the nurse in charge".
   A printed page carries its words for ever; a kiosk view recomputes them every time it is shown.
   Every page also carries the print time and the back-entry instruction. The CSO confirms the
   figure and all four wordings (H-101).
3. **Who is in it.** Everyone receiving care at the location: active, on leave and in hospital
   (their pages say "On leave" or "In hospital"). Discharged, deceased, archived and not-admitted
   people, and people at another location, are left out with the reason in the internal record. A
   person is never left out because their record is incomplete; a missing date of birth is a
   warning. Pages follow the building: room in natural order, then name; no room last.
4. **Wrong person** (HZ-001). A section whose data belongs to another person is withheld, never
   printed: "Not included: this information did not match the person. Check the record when
   Harbour is back." Another person's contact is dropped and the person's own contacts still print.
   The whole pack is never refused for one person's problem: an outage without any pack is worse.
5. **Resuscitation** (HZ-015). Every active ReSPECT and DNACPR decision prints, newest first, with
   its clinician, date and review date; Harbour never picks a "current" one. When the decisions in
   force disagree the section's state is `conflicting` and the words inside the section say:
   "Resuscitation records disagree. Every record in force is listed here. Harbour does not choose
   between them: check the signed forms and follow the home's policy." (wording for the CSO,
   H-100). A decision past its review date still prints and is noted.
6. **Unreadable values** (HZ-016). A stored IDDSI code that is not an IDDSI level makes that section
   `unreadable`: "This could not be read from the record. Check the record before giving food or
   drink.", with a warning; the rest of the page and the pack still print.
7. **Placeholders** (typed ports). Until PEEPs (Phase 3) and the eMAR (Phase 2) exist, the PEEP and
   medicines sections say where the location keeps them, from its settings
   (`paperMarMedicinesPort(location)`, `fireFileEvacuationPort(location)`; seeded with
   `DEFAULT_PAPER_MAR_LOCATION` and `DEFAULT_PEEP_LOCATION`, which the Registered Manager replaces
   with the real place). The Phase 2 medicines port already carries scheduled doses with their
   status (`due`, `given`, `not_given`) and given time, and PRN medicines with their last-given
   time: only doses still due from `as_at` to `valid_until` print, in time order with time-critical
   medicines first (HZ-003). HZ-021's residual risk while these are pointers only is a CSO decision
   (H-102).
8. **Generation and the kiosk.** The worker builds every location's pack nightly; a holder of
   `operations.downtime.read` can build one on demand. A kiosk caches the latest pack and can build
   one from its own cache when the server cannot be reached, with `as_at` its last sync. The cached
   pack holds every person at the location, wider than any shared-device user's scope, so
   (`kiosk-cache.ts`; [kiosk.md](../compliance/kiosk.md) section 3.5): it is encrypted under a key
   the device releases only to a PIN session of a holder of `operations.downtime.read`
   (`kioskDowntimePackUnlock`); every print or open is queued as an audit event with ids only and
   uploaded when online (`kioskPrintAuditEvent`); it is evicted when the device key is revoked and
   when its `downtime_pack` retention ends (`kioskDowntimeCacheDisposition`). Where the printed pack
   is kept and who prints it is a deployer control in the business continuity plan.
9. **Declaring an outage** (`checkOutageDeclaration`). A kind and a description of at least
   `MIN_OUTAGE_DESCRIPTION_LENGTH` characters. The start may be before the declaration and an outage
   may be declared after it ended (with its end), so that back-entries have a window; a declaration
   more than `care_note.late_entry_hours` hours after the start is flagged. Neither start nor end may
   be in the future beyond `sync.clock_drift_flag_minutes`, and the end is not before the start.
   Outage windows at a location never overlap: an earlier outage can be declared late beside a
   later one when they do not overlap.
10. **Closing** (`checkOutageClosure`). The end is not before the start and not in the future. The
    back-entry deadline is the closure plus `downtime.back_entry_due_hours`; overdue back-entry
    escalates to the Registered Manager (the closed state's SLA in `OUTAGE_WORKFLOW`).
11. **Paper times** (`resolvePaperTime`; HZ-009, HZ-017). The date and time written on the form are
    resolved in UK time with the one rule for typed event times (`resolveRecordedUkTime`): a time in
    the hour the clocks skip in spring (01:00 to 01:59 on the last Sunday in March) is refused; a
    time in the hour repeated in autumn asks which of the two it was and never guesses; a future
    time is refused. `checkBackEntry` takes the result.
12. **Back-entry** (`checkBackEntry`; HZ-017). A record written on paper is entered with the paper
    time as its event time, the late-entry reason `downtime` and the outage it belongs to; the
    outage must be at the person's location and not cancelled. A paper time outside the outage
    window (start to end, or to the time of entry while the outage is open) is refused unless a
    reason of at least `MIN_BACK_ENTRY_REASON_LENGTH` characters is given, and the reason is shown
    with the entry. The usual late-entry flag (care.md rule 3) still applies, and every back-entry
    carries a label wherever it appears: "Written on paper 11/10/2026 15:05 during the outage (power
    cut) from 11/10/2026 14:40. Entered in Harbour 11/10/2026 19:20 on the device, received
    11/10/2026 19:21." Back-entries refresh the handovers of the shift they happened in and the
    shift that received them (care.md rule 8). Corrections supersede, as for any care event.
13. **Through the normal route** (`checkEntryAgainstOutages`; HZ-017). Every care write checks its
    event time against the location's declared outages. Written at the time on a device that kept
    working offline, it is ordinary. Written later, for a time inside an outage, it must either be
    entered as a back-entry or say why it was not written on paper; so a copied paper form never
    goes in without its label.
14. **Duplicates and reconciliation** (`possibleDuplicateBackEntries`,
    `checkOutageReconciliation`; HZ-017). Back-entries are compared with every entry for the same
    person and kind near the outage, digital entries recorded offline included, and listed as
    possible duplicates when within `downtime.duplicate_match_minutes` of each other, unless two
    paper forms' references show they are different forms. Someone who entered none of the paper
    records confirms the outage reconciled, only after reviewing each possible duplicate (kept, or
    corrected by supersession) and writing what was checked. The summary counts back-entries, the
    forms entered (distinct references), entries without a reference, entries outside the window,
    people, and digital entries inside the window.
15. **Cancelling** an outage declared in error needs a reason and is refused by the API once any
    back-entry cites it.

## 5. API, audit events and outbox events

For the schema and API step (none of this is built in slice 11):

- `GET /v1/locations/{id}/downtime-pack` (latest pack with its freshness; `?format=pdf` renders it
  with the page header from rule 2 on every page), `POST /v1/locations/{id}/downtime-pack`
  (build now). Worker job: nightly per location, idempotent per location and night.
- `GET` and `POST /v1/locations/{id}/outages` (declare), `POST /v1/outages/{id}/close`,
  `/reconcile` (statement and reviewed entry ids), `/cancel` (reason).
- Every care write route runs `checkEntryAgainstOutages`; a back-entry carries a `backEntry` object
  (`outageId`, the paper date and time as written, the occurrence chosen for a repeated hour,
  `reason`, `paperReference`) and the server runs `resolvePaperTime` and `checkBackEntry`.
- Audit events: `operations.downtime_pack.generated`, `.printed`, `.opened`, `.cached` (with the
  device; kiosk prints and opens arrive from the device's queue), `operations.outage.declared`,
  `.closed`, `.reconciled`, `.cancelled`; back-entries are audited as care writes with the outage id.
- Outbox events: `operations.outage.declared` (the notification centre tells the Registered
  Manager), `operations.outage.closed` (raises the back-entry task), `operations.outage.reconciled`,
  `operations.downtime_pack.generated` (kiosks fetch it).

## 6. Offline behaviour (section 17.1)

| Data | On the device | Writes offline | Conflict handling |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Latest downtime pack (JSON and PDF) for the location | Kiosk devices at the location, encrypted, opened only in a PIN session of an `operations.downtime.read` holder; replaced when a newer one arrives; evicted on device revocation or at the end of retention | Printing only, each print queued as an audit event | The page header is recomputed at print time from the device clock; a clock behind the pack is flagged; a pack built from the cache shows its last sync as `as_at` |
| Outages at the location | Devices at the location, read only | Declaring needs the server | Not applicable |
| Back-entries and care entries for times inside an outage | As the care event | Yes, queued with the outage id | Append-only; possible duplicates, paper and digital, are reviewed at reconciliation |

## 7. Clinical safety

Hazards: HZ-021 (no access to essential information during an IT outage, extended with the
out-of-date pack cause and the freshness controls), HZ-017 (late or retrospective entry mistaken for
a contemporaneous record, extended with back-entry from paper), HZ-009 (paper times written during a
clock change), HZ-001 (wrong-person sections withheld), HZ-003 (medicines due), HZ-007, HZ-015,
HZ-016 and HZ-024. See the [hazard log](../clinical-safety/hazard-log.md). Medical-device boundary:
the pack copies recorded information and says what is missing, unreadable or held elsewhere; it
prints every disagreeing resuscitation record without choosing between them; nothing scores,
predicts or recommends ([medical-device-boundary.md](../compliance/medical-device-boundary.md)).

## 8. Data protection and data standards

A downtime pack holds special category data for everyone at a location, on paper as well as on
screen (records of processing: downtime packs and outages, purpose B1). Printed packs are a
deployer control: kept where the business continuity plan says, and superseded packs shredded; the
kiosk copy is encrypted, PIN-gated and evicted as rule 8 says. Retention: `downtime_pack` for
nightly packs, `downtime_record` for outages and the packs in force during them
([retention.md](retention.md)); back-entries stay `care_record`. Field mapping:
[data-standards.md](data-standards.md#packs-and-governance-phase-1-slice-11).

## 12. As built (slice 11 part 1, schema)

Migrations 0050 and 0051; `packages/db/src/schema/operations.ts`; tests in
`packages/db/test/downtime.test.ts`. No API yet.

- `downtime_pack`: append-only (INSERT and SELECT for the application, `append_only` triggers).
  `person_ids` lists the people on its pages, worked out by a trigger whatever the caller sends
  (#32 review round 2). The stored content names no one else: only the pack's own keys, an empty
  or absent `excluded`, and warnings only about people on its pages; the `warnings` column holds
  counts only. A pack without its pages, a page naming no one, or a pack with no pages while
  anyone is receiving care at the location, is refused. Visible only in tenant scope or location
  scope at its location, when the reader can see every person on it
  (`app.downtime_pack_visible`; a person who has since moved away hides the pack from the old
  location), and, for a member, only where they hold `operations.downtime.read`; a member builds one only in their own name, and the nightly
  pack (`server_nightly`, no `generated_by`) only the system. Retention `downtime_pack`; a pack in
  force during a declared outage is kept as `downtime_record`, worked out from the outages at its
  location (the row is append-only, so no class is stored on it).
- `outage`: mutable and versioned (`touch_mutable`, `capture_version`; the API checks
  `expected_version`). Visible in tenant scope, location scope at its location, and to anyone who
  can see a person there (`app.outage_visible`); declared, closed, reconciled and cancelled only by
  holders of `operations.downtime.manage` at the location. An exclusion constraint keeps windows at
  a location from overlapping (cancelled ones aside). `app.outage_declare` declares it open, with
  nothing closed, reconciled or cancelled, at the database's time, and works out `declared_late`
  from `care_note.late_entry_hours` (late when no figure is found). `app.outage_guard` keeps the
  location and declaration, moves the state only along `OUTAGE_WORKFLOW` in the name of the member
  moving it, sets the closure once when it closes, lets the start and end be corrected before
  reconciliation only with a `correction_reason` of their own (an open outage stays open; the late
  flag follows a corrected start), makes reconciled and cancelled outages final, and refuses a soft
  delete. Retention `downtime_record`.
- Location settings `downtime.mar_location` and `downtime.peep_location` are the columns
  `location.downtime_mar_location` and `location.downtime_peep_location`, seeded with
  `DEFAULT_PAPER_MAR_LOCATION` and `DEFAULT_PEEP_LOCATION` and never blank. Only a holder of
  `platform.location.manage` at the location (or a job with no member) changes or confirms them; a
  confirmation (`downtime_places_confirmed_at`, `_by`) is in the confirmer's own name, and a place
  changed without one is unconfirmed again (H-201).
- Part 2 (migration 0053; `packages/db/test/back-entry.test.ts`): the expand-only back-entry
  columns on `care_note`, `observation`, `body_map_mark`, `activity_record` and `visit_log`
  (`late_entry_reason`, `outage_id`, `paper_date`, `paper_time`, `paper_occurrence`,
  `outside_outage_reason`, `paper_reference`, `not_from_paper_reason`), empty on every entry written
  at the time. A CHECK ties them together: a back-entry (`downtime`) has its outage, the date and
  time written (to the minute) and the occurrence, and no not-from-paper reason; any other entry
  has none of the paper details, and names an outage only with a reason it was not from paper.
  `app.entry_outage_guard` refuses an outage at another location, a cancelled one, or one the
  writer cannot see. `app.outage_guard` refuses to cancel an outage once a back-entry cites it
  (rule 15), a reconciler who entered any of its back-entries (rule 14), and a correction of the
  start or end that would leave a back-entry with no outside-the-outage reason outside it (#32
  review round 2), all counted in the organisation's view (`app.outage_back_entries`), so a
  back-entry about someone the caller can no longer see still counts.
- Part 2 after #38 review round 1:
  - `app.entry_outage_guard` also refuses a reconciled outage, and reads the outage again locked
    for share (in the system's view, as a share lock needs the update policy), so a cancel or a
    reconciliation at the same moment waits for the entry and then counts it.
  - A back-entry's `occurred_at` is the instant its paper date and time give in Europe/London
    (`app.paper_instant`): in the repeated autumn hour `earlier` is British Summer Time and
    `later` Greenwich Mean Time, and `only` is refused; a time in the skipped spring hour is
    refused.
  - The outage window is `[started_at, ended_at)` (the end outside, as for the no-overlap rule),
    an open outage running to now plus `sync.clock_drift_flag_minutes`. A back-entry outside it
    needs `outside_outage_reason`; an entry not from paper may name only an outage its time falls
    in. The window is checked on every entry and whenever the start or end changes (closing as
    revised in round 2, below).
  - A correction (supersession) of a back-entry stays a back-entry of the same outage: its paper
    details are carried from the entry it corrects when it gives none, and it is refused when it
    names another outage or none.
  - `app.outage_back_entries` says whether each entry is a correction and whether it has been
    corrected: superseded entries do not count for the cancel and window checks (H-205), and the
    reconciler must not be the author, or the sender (`handed_over_by`), of any original
    back-entry, while someone who only corrected one may reconcile (H-203).
  - Not checked in the database: rule 13 (the normal route), which depends on the API applying
    `checkEntryAgainstOutages` on every care route and on the sync upload.
- Part 2 after #38 review round 2:
  - A correction of an entry that cites the outage, a back-entry or one not from paper, is
    accepted after the outage is reconciled, keeping the link (a back-entry keeps its paper details
    too); only new entries citing it are refused. A wrong value (1500 ml for 150 ml) can always be
    corrected.
  - A back-entry is corrected once: a partial unique index on `(tenant_id, supersedes_id)` over
    back-entry rows on each of the five entries means a further correction supersedes the
    correction, so each paper record has one current version. Ordinary entries keep concurrent
    offline corrections.
  - For the reconciler check, a correction is only an entry whose original is a back-entry of the
    same outage, and a correction cannot make an entry not entered from paper a back-entry.
  - Closing may set the true end before back-entries already entered (H-207). Reconciliation is
    then refused until each current back-entry outside the window with no reason for it is in
    `reviewed_entry_ids`, so the reconciler looks at each one. A correction of the start or end is
    refused only when it would leave outside a back-entry that was inside the window before.
- Not yet: the printed pack (part 3a-2); the nightly job, rebuilds after moves and the places
  route (part 3b); outages and back-entry through the API (part 3c); outages and packs in the
  sync streams, and the kiosk fetch (with the mobile step).
- Part 3 requirements (#32 review round 1):
  - A pack is hidden from the location's holders once someone it names moves away (H-200). The
    API builds a fresh pack on demand whenever anyone moves into, out of or away from the
    location, and on a read when the latest pack the reader can open is not the location's
    latest, so a holder at a reachable location is never left without one (`hazard HZ-021
    rebuilds the pack when someone on the latest one has moved`). The kiosk fetches the latest

## 13. As built (slice 11 part 3a, downtime pack API)

`apps/api/src/routes/operations.ts`, `services/downtime-pack.ts`; tests in
`apps/api/test/downtime.test.ts`.

- `GET /v1/locations/{id}/downtime-pack` (`operations.downtime.read` where the location is): the
  latest pack the reader can open, built on the spot as `server_on_demand` when it no longer serves:
  it does not name exactly the people receiving care there with their status now, anything it was
  built from has changed since its information's time, or it has expired. So a pack hidden because
  someone on it moved away is replaced for a holder at a reachable location. The freshness words
  and the four header lines are worked out at the moment of the request (`downtimePackFreshness`,
  `downtimePageHeader`, with any outage open at the location; during an outage, "anything recorded
  since" is the earlier of the information's time and the outage's start). Opening and building
  are audited (`operations.downtime_pack.opened`, `.generated`); a location out of reach reads as
  not found and is audited as denied.
- What a pack is built from (`sourcesChangedSince`; #45 review round 1, H1): the people (identity,
  room, location, status, photo), their allergies, advance care records, eating and drinking
  profile, contacts, care plans and sections, risk assessments and photo documents, and the
  location (the places), soft deletes included. The information's time is the database's clock
  before anything is read, and the comparison runs in the database to the microsecond, so a new
  DNACPR, allergy or room swap after it means a new pack.
- One build at a time per location (an advisory lock around the check and the build): a second
  reader waits, then gets the pack just built. If a build fails, the latest pack is served with
  `rebuildFailed` and a header line saying it could not be rebuilt and when it was made, the
  failure is audited (outcome `error`) and logged by opaque id; with no pack at all the answer is
  503 with what to use instead, never a bare error.
- `POST /v1/locations/{id}/downtime-pack`: builds one now unless the latest still serves (`built`
  says which). It needs a fresh sign-in (section 12, bulk export). Both routes count against
  `downtime_pack.requests_per_hour` per member (429 with Retry-After beyond it).
- Building reads only the location's own people receiving care, in the builder's view, and
  publishes `operations.downtime_pack.generated` (location and pack ids) for kiosks. While the MAR
  and PEEP places are unconfirmed, the pack prints "where it is kept is not set in Harbour: ask the
  nurse in charge" (H-201). Allergies are each person's own current ones: inactive, resolved,
  refuted, entered in error and deleted rows never print, as on the phone (`currentAllergiesOf`,
  shared with the hospital transfer pack; HZ-007).
